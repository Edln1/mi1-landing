FIELD NOTES / 01Voice & ideas

Good ideas start
with a voice.

From a passing thought to something you can return to.

A dedicated recorder gives a conversation a place to live. The microphone captures sound; software can turn that recording into words and then organise those words into something easier to revisit.

The future, in everyday objects.
UNDER THE SURFACE

Three steps. Different jobs.

Recording, transcription and summarisation are separate stages. A recording stores the sound itself. Speech recognition estimates the words. A summariser selects and reorganises information. Keeping the original audio gives you something to check when a name, number or nuance matters.

A sound. A signal. A thought worth keeping.

FIELD NOTES / 02Wearable displays

A little information.
A different perspective.

Understanding what a display in a pair of glasses can actually do.

The most interesting interface might be the one that asks for less attention. A tiny display and carefully designed optics can put a short prompt into your field of view while the world around you stays visible.

The future, in everyday objects.
UNDER THE SURFACE

Small display. Carefully directed light.

In the AR99 reference platform, a micro-LED light engine works with a waveguide to send a single-green image to the right eye. The glasses connect to a phone over Bluetooth. The hardware presents the information; the paired software determines which information is available.

FIELD NOTES / 03Spatial displays

A nostalgic touch.
Another dimension.

A familiar frame, with an unfamiliar sense of depth.

A picture frame is already a small window onto somewhere else. A multiview display adds another ingredient: different perspectives, directed toward different viewing positions.

The future, in everyday objects.
UNDER THE SURFACE

The picture changes with your point of view.

A light-field display presents multiple views of suitable content. As your position changes, you see a different perspective. The impression of depth belongs to the display and its viewing zone. The effect depends on the source content, optics and where you stand.

FIELD NOTES / 04Companion robotics

A little character.
A place in your day.

What makes a moving camera different from a desktop companion?

A face, a voice and a little movement can change how a piece of technology feels. But “companion robot” covers very different objects. Start with its physical job: does it move through a room, stay on your desk, or offer a voice interface?

The future, in everyday objects.
UNDER THE SURFACE

Hardware gives personality a place to live.

A mobile home camera combines a view of the room with movement and communication. A desktop companion focuses on nearby interaction: a display, touch input, audio or a cloud conversation service. Animated expressions make an interface approachable; they do not establish that a device has feelings.

No. Stationary desktop models and mobile camera robots have different hardware. Even movement does not guarantee autonomous mapping or obstacle handling. Check the specific model rather than assuming a capability from its appearance.
